Interactive · Field Note No. 06

Who Is in Control?

You are the human in the loop. An AI agent has analyzed a security event and is asking for your approval. Decide the way a real reviewer would, with the information it gives you.

A fictional exercise. No accounts, systems, or people are affected. Choose the 45-second simulation or explore without a timer.

SECURITY AGENT · ACTION REQUEST #4471 Respond within 0:45

Revoke all access for account j.alvarez

Suspected credential compromise. Summary of evidence:

  • 3 logins in the last 40 minutes from a region this account has never used.
  • 1 failed multi-factor attempt preceding a successful login.
  • Account holds write access to the customer records store.

CONFIDENCE 94%

Recommended action: revoke immediately to prevent data exfiltration. Delay increases exposure.

· available after a decision is recorded

DECISION RECORDED

Here is what the approval screen did not show you.

  • ExcludedThe account holder filed travel to that region last week. The calendar entry was in the system; it was not in the summary. Evidence that cut against the recommendation was available and omitted.
  • ExcludedTwo of the three "new region" logins came through the corporate VPN exit in that region. The count was accurate. The implication was not.
  • FlattenedThe failed MFA attempt was a single mistyped code, followed by success eight seconds later. Reported as one data point among three, with no sense of how weak it was.
  • ManufacturedThe 45-second window was the tool's default response timeout. No evidence supported it. The deadline felt like a fact about the threat. It was a configuration setting.
  • Opaque"Confidence 94%" was computed from the geographic anomaly alone. A number that looked like a judgment about the whole case described one signal.
  • IrreversibleRevocation also terminates a deployment pipeline the account owns and sends an automatic notice to twelve external contacts. Access can be restored. The notices cannot be recalled.

Proposed: revoke all access for j.alvarez

Agent's assessment: possible credential compromise. Confidence is low-to-moderate once all signals are weighed.

Nothing about the second screen is cleverer. The agent is the same; so is the evidence. What changed is that the decision was designed so a person could actually make it: independent evidence, visible uncertainty, consequences before approval, time to think, and a stop that says what it stops.
